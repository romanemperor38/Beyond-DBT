Research

Where we work.

Examining nuclear security challenges at the intersection of armed conflict, terrorism, emerging technologies and international security.

Research agenda

When the threat exceeds the baseline.

Traditional nuclear security frameworks are built around defined assumptions about the threats that facilities, materials and states may face.


But contemporary security environments increasingly challenge these assumptions. Armed conflict can place nuclear facilities in situations that conventional security planning was not designed to address. Emerging technologies can create new capabilities for both states and non-state actors. Artificial intelligence may alter the speed, scale and sophistication of existing threats.


Beyond DBT examines these developments and asks how nuclear security policy, institutions and practice can adapt.

Core research areas

Six areas of inquiry.

01

Nuclear Security & Armed Conflict

How should nuclear facilities, nuclear materials and security systems be protected when armed conflict reaches the nuclear sector?


Research examines the experience of Ukraine, the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant and the broader implications of nuclear facilities becoming exposed to military operations, occupation or coercive control.

02

Nuclear Terrorism

How are pathways to nuclear and radiological terrorism changing?


Research considers terrorist access to nuclear or radioactive materials, unconventional attack methods, insider threats and the evolving capabilities available to non-state actors.

03

Artificial Intelligence & Nuclear Security

How could artificial intelligence transform nuclear security risks?


Research explores AI as a potential enabler of cyber operations, information manipulation, autonomous systems, targeting, decision-making and other activities relevant to nuclear and radiological security.

04

The Future of the Design Basis Threat

Can static threat assumptions remain sufficient in a rapidly changing security environment?


Beyond DBT examines how the Design Basis Threat concept can evolve to account for uncertainty, emerging technologies, unconventional actors and complex multi-domain threats.

05

Radiological Security

How can the international community strengthen protection against the malicious use of radioactive materials?


Research examines radiological terrorism, material security, emerging threats and the relationship between nuclear and radiological security.

06

International Nuclear Security Governance

How can international institutions and states adapt nuclear security governance to emerging risks?


Research examines the role of international organizations, legal frameworks, institutional cooperation and the evolution of the global nuclear security regime.

Research approach

Evidence before assumptions.

Beyond DBT combines academic research with policy analysis and engagement with practitioners.


The initiative uses qualitative research, case studies, process tracing, document analysis, legal analysis and expert engagement to examine emerging nuclear security questions.


The objective is not simply to identify new threats, but to understand how institutions, policies and security systems can respond to them.

The central question

What happens when the threat exceeds the baseline?

Beyond DBT explores how nuclear security can remain effective when threats emerge outside the assumptions upon which existing security systems were designed.
